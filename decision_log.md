# Decision log

Format: **Decision | Reason | Evidence (count)**. Counts come from `reports/cleaning_log.csv` (rules applied in order; each row counted under the first rule that removed it). Raw rows before rule 1: 1,067,371.

## DE2 — cleaning (`src/clean.py`, `notebooks/02_clean.ipynb`)

- Rule 0 — Normalise `StockCode` to str/strip/upper, `Invoice` to str/strip, `Description` to str; leave `Customer ID` as float until rule 7 | Raw columns mix Python `int` and `str` (932,385 int vs 134,986 str StockCodes; 1,047,871 int vs 19,500 str Invoices; 4 int Descriptions), so string ops and parquet writing need one type; lower-case `m` / `gift_0001_*` are the same codes as `M` / `GIFT_0001_*` | 0 rows removed
- Rule 1 — Drop `Year 2010-2011` rows with `InvoiceDate <= 2010-12-09 20:01:00` (max date of `Year 2009-2010`), then concatenate | DE1 showed the two sheets overlap for 9 days and every row in that window is an exact copy of a row in the earlier sheet | 22,523 rows removed (1,067,371 -> 1,044,848)
- Rule 2 — Drop exact duplicate rows (all columns equal), keep first | A line repeated inside the same invoice at the same timestamp/price is an export artefact, not two purchases | 11,812 rows removed (-> 1,033,036)
- Rule 3 — Drop cancellation invoices (`Invoice` starts with `C`) | Cancellations are negative-quantity reversals, not product interest | 19,104 rows removed, 8,292 distinct `C` invoices (-> 1,013,932)
- Cancellations removed but original purchases kept: a bought-then-returned item still signals product interest for recommendations. | Netting a return against its purchase would erase evidence that the customer chose that product; for a recommender the choice is the signal | 19,104 cancellation rows dropped, 0 original purchase rows touched
- Rule 4 — Drop `Quantity <= 0` | After rule 3 these are non-cancellation stock adjustments / damaged items with no customer purchase behind them | 3,393 rows removed (-> 1,010,539); 3,393 were not on a `C` invoice before rule 3 (DE1 counted 3,457 on raw data before overlap/duplicate removal); no `Quantity == 0` rows exist
- Rule 5 — Drop `Price <= 0` | Zero price is a free item or missing price, negative price is an accounting adjustment; neither is a real purchase | 2,626 rows removed (2,621 at price 0, 5 negative) (-> 1,007,913)
- Rule 6 — Drop non-product `StockCode`s: `POST, DOT, M, D, S, B, C2, BANK CHARGES, ADJUST, ADJUST2, AMAZONFEE, CRUK, TEST001, TEST002` and any code starting with `GIFT_`; keep `DCGS*` and `PADS` | Postage, carriage, manual lines, discounts, samples, bad-debt/bank/Amazon fees, commission, gift vouchers and test rows are not products a customer could be recommended; `DCGS*` and `PADS` have real product descriptions | 4,556 rows removed (-> 1,003,357); 38 non-standard codes reviewed, 20 dropped, 18 kept; `CRUK` had 0 rows left by this stage
- Rule 6 follow-up — `ADJUST2` (3 rows, "Adjustment by Peter on Jun 25 2010") and `TEST002` (1 row, "This is a test product.") added to the exclusion list after the first review; `SP1002` (2 rows, "KID'S CHALKBOARD/EASEL") kept | `ADJUST2` is a manual adjustment and `TEST002` a test row, i.e. the same kind of non-product as `ADJUST` / `TEST001`; `SP1002` is a real product with a real description, and the DE4 popularity filter (fitted on training data only) decides whether such rare items survive | 4 extra rows removed by rule 6; SP1002's 2 rows kept
- Rule 7 — Drop rows with missing `Customer ID`, then cast `Customer ID` to int64 | A customer x product matrix needs a customer on every row; IDs are whole numbers stored as float only because of the NaNs | 226,761 rows removed (-> 776,596 final rows; 5,852 customers, 4,621 products, 36,594 invoices, 2009-12-01 to 2011-12-09)

## DE3 — time-based split (`src/split.py`, `notebooks/03_split.ipynb`)

- Cutoff = `(max InvoiceDate - 2 months).normalize()` = **2011-10-09 00:00:00**; train = rows before it, test period = rows on/after it | The brief specifies the last 2 months as the hold-out; a purely temporal split mimics deployment (recommend from past purchases, score on future ones) and avoids leaking future behaviour into training. Normalising to midnight makes the boundary a calendar date | Train 659,830 rows (2009-12-01 to 2011-10-07; 5,472 customers, 4,531 products); test period 116,766 rows (2011-10-09 to 2011-12-09; 2,423 customers). All leakage assertions pass
- Repeat purchases excluded from test targets: a target is a `(customer, StockCode)` pair the customer never bought in train | The recommender's job is discovery ("customers like you also bought"); re-buys are trivially predictable from the customer's own history and would inflate every method's score without measuring the thing we care about | 95,278 distinct test pairs, of which 31,791 (33%) were repeats and were dropped
- Cold-start customers (in the test period but not in train) excluded from evaluation; count reported | They have no row in the customer x product matrix, so neither the SVD model nor any personalised method can score them; the popularity baseline could, but comparing on different customer sets would be unfair | 380 cold-start customers excluded (13,840 new pairs dropped with them). A further 112 customers were in train but had only repeat purchases in the test period, so they have no targets and are also not evaluated. Evaluation set: 1,931 customers, 49,647 target pairs (mean 25.7, median 17 per customer)
- No product popularity filter applied in the split; each target is flagged `seen_in_train` instead | Filtering products belongs to DE4 and must be fitted on train only; the flag lets us report the share of targets no train-based model could ever hit | 3,670 target pairs (7.39%) are for products never sold in train; 14 evaluation customers have only such targets
- Risk: Test period is the Oct-Dec Christmas peak; seasonal demand may favour or hurt both model and baseline. Flag in memo. | Christmas-themed items dominate late-year sales and appear only partially in the train window's two earlier Decembers, so both the SVD factors and the popularity list may under- or over-weight seasonal items | Test window 2011-10-09 to 2011-12-09

## DE4/DE5 — customer x product matrix (`src/build_matrix.py`, `notebooks/04_matrix.ipynb`, `tests/test_build_matrix.py`)

- Aggregate `Quantity` per `(Customer ID, StockCode)` into `total_qty` **before** any transform; build `csr_matrix` from the aggregated table and assert `nnz == number of pairs` | Applying `log1p` per invoice line and letting `csr_matrix` sum duplicates compounds the transform: two orders of 12 give ln(13)+ln(13) = ln(169) ~ 5.13 instead of ln(25) ~ 3.22. On real data one example customer x product 82494L has 112 lines totalling 906 units: correct cell 6.81, per-line mistake 242.35 | 659,830 train lines -> 417,501 unique pairs; `nnz` = 413,706 = pairs after filtering (assertion passes); unit test `tests/test_build_matrix.py` checks the ln(25)-not-ln(169) case explicitly (8 tests pass)
- Product filter `min_customers = 10` (distinct customers in train), fitted on train only | Products bought by fewer than 10 customers carry almost no co-purchase signal for SVD and inflate the column count (and noise) for little gain; 10 removes ~19% of products but < 1% of pairs, and no evaluation customer loses their row. It is computed from train, so no test information leaks into which products exist | 4,531 -> 3,687 products (844 dropped); 417,501 -> 413,706 pairs (3,795 removed, 0.91%)
- `log` (`log1p(total_qty)`) is the main purchase value; `binary` (1.0) and `raw` (`total_qty`) are built on the identical sparsity pattern and saved for the ML7 sensitivity check | Raw quantities span 1 to 74,215 and would let wholesale buyers dominate the factors; binary throws away repeat-intensity entirely; `log1p` compresses the range (0.69 to 11.21) while keeping order. Building all three with the same pattern means the sensitivity check isolates the value transform | `A_log` values min 0.693 / median 1.946 / max 11.215; `A_raw` max 74,215
- Drop customers with no rows after the product filter | A row of all zeros has no information and would break per-row normalisation; the customer can be scored by the popularity baseline only, so they are excluded consistently | 1 customer dropped (one customer who bought only `84760L`, a product with < 10 customers); 0 of the 1,931 evaluation customers affected
- Hit-rate ceiling from `in_matrix` flag on test targets | Only targets whose customer row and product column exist in the matrix can be recommended by any model built on it; the share of evaluation customers with at least one such target bounds hit rate from above | 44,922 of 49,647 target pairs in matrix (90.48%); 4,725 out (3,670 products never seen in train + 1,055 seen but under the 10-customer filter); 1,913 of 1,931 evaluation customers (99.07%) have at least one reachable target
- Matrix size and storage (DE5) | Keeping the matrix sparse is what makes SVD on 5,471 x 3,687 practical; recorded for the memo | shape 5,471 x 3,687, nnz 413,706, sparsity 97.95%, dense 161.4 MB vs sparse 5.0 MB (32x compression); products per customer min 1 / median 41 / max 2,255; customers per product min 10 / median 66 / max 1,422 (`reports/matrix_report.csv`)
- Matrix frozen on 2026-10-13 | No cleaning, split, or matrix changes after this point; all ML results use data/processed/A_log.npz as rebuilt by build_matrix.py | build_matrix.py rebuild is byte-identical to notebook outputs
